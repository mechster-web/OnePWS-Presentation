# Interactive Control Room Presentation Experience Strategy

This document defines the creative and experience strategy for the new Pyrotech Workspace Control Room presentation. It is based on the existing PDF analysis in `docs/existing-presentation-analysis.md`.

The product should not behave like a slide deck or a corporate website. It should feel like entering a premium mission-control environment: quiet, precise, cinematic, tactile and guided, with enough freedom for a visitor to explore and enough structure for a salesperson to close a serious conversation.

## Experience intent

The experience should make a buyer feel three things in sequence:

1. Pyrotech understands the operational seriousness of control rooms.
2. Pyrotech can design, manufacture and deliver the complete environment, not only furniture.
3. Pyrotech has already done this at scale for high-trust customers across transport, smart cities, government, defense, energy, utilities and industrial sectors.

The emotional tone should be calm authority. No shouting, no brochure-like blocks, no generic hero sections, no dense logo walls as primary storytelling. Every screen should feel like it belongs inside a premium showroom, an executive briefing room or a large-format control-room display.

## Experience modes

### Presenter Mode

Presenter Mode is for a salesperson leading a client conversation.

- The presenter controls chapter progression manually with keyboard, remote clicker, touchscreen or discreet on-screen controls.
- Each chapter has a primary narrative path plus optional proof layers: metrics, project details, logos, partner claims and technical notes.
- The presenter can jump directly to sector-specific proof, such as Smart City, Rail/Metro, Oil & Gas, Defense/Aerospace, Utilities or Airport/ATC.
- Voice narration is off by default, but short scripted presenter notes can be available in a hidden presenter panel.
- The experience should support “pause and inspect” moments: freeze a 3D/architectural scene, rotate a console module, expand access-floor layers, or open project details.
- Sensitive items such as project values and full logo lists should be toggleable or protected behind a “sales proof” layer, because these may not suit every audience.

### Self-Guided Mode

Self-Guided Mode is for showroom visitors exploring independently.

- The experience begins with a short ambient intro, then offers clear chapter choices without looking like a website menu.
- Navigation should feel like a control-room interface: sector tiles, project pins, product modules and “explore system” hotspots.
- Every chapter must have an obvious next action, but visitors should never be trapped in a linear sequence.
- Narration is optional and can be toggled on with one control.
- Product exploration should be tactile: rotate, zoom, reveal layers, compare workstation sightlines, inspect cable routing and access floor zones.
- Idle behavior should gracefully return to an ambient overview after inactivity, then continue into Auto-Play Mode if configured.

### Auto-Play Mode

Auto-Play Mode is for unattended showroom loops, trade fairs and large displays.

- The full experience runs automatically with narration, cinematic transitions and timed chapter beats.
- Auto-play should never depend on small text. It should use large visuals, short captions, spoken narration and animated proof points.
- A visitor can interrupt auto-play at any time by touching, clicking or pressing a key. The system then enters Self-Guided Mode at the current chapter.
- After inactivity, the experience can resume auto-play from the next chapter or restart from the beginning, configurable per installation.
- Auto-play should include a shorter showroom loop option of 3-5 minutes and a full sales loop of 10-14 minutes.

## Experience principles

### Mission-critical first

Open with the seriousness of control rooms: operators, live data, visibility, response and uptime. Company facts should appear after the buyer understands the mission context.

### One idea per moment

Each screen should carry one decisive thought: scale, design-build, ergonomics, access-floor integration, flagship proof or partner credibility. Dense tables and logo walls should become optional detail.

### Proof emerges from the environment

Facts should not sit in generic stat cards. They should appear as overlays inside control-room scenes: 400+ delivered solutions over a project wall, 170,000 sq. ft. factory inside manufacturing context, 25-country design-build presence on an interactive map.

### The room is the product

The experience should sell the complete mission-critical environment: console, operator posture, video wall, lighting, HVAC, access floor, cable management, acoustics, room geometry, cladding and long-term support.

### Guided, not locked

The story has a recommended sequence, but the buyer or presenter can branch to proof, sectors, projects or product details. The system should feel controlled, not linear.

### Premium restraint

Use fewer words, better imagery and intentional motion. Avoid corporate gradients, decorative graphics, stock-photo layouts and generic “solutions” sections.

## Visual language

### Overall look

- Dark precision interface with controlled contrast, inspired by control-room lighting and executive product launches.
- Primary environments should be full-bleed: control room, showroom, console close-up, architectural interior, access-floor cutaway, world map.
- Use white, graphite, charcoal, muted metallics and controlled amber/yellow accents from the Work Space identity.
- Use blue/cyan only as data/display illumination, not as a generic corporate color wash.
- Keep the UI quiet: thin dividers, glass-like overlays only where they improve legibility, clear spacing, no stacked cards.

### Typography

- Large chapter titles should be short and cinematic: “Designed for Decisions,” “Built as a System,” “400+ Control Rooms,” “Proof Across Critical Sectors.”
- Body copy should be minimal, usually one or two lines.
- Technical labels should feel like instrument annotations: sightline, operator reach, cable void, HVAC zone, lux level, console module, access floor.
- Metrics should be oversized and sparse, with exact source facts from the PDF only.

### Imagery

- Use real project imagery from the existing deck wherever approved: WDFCC Ahmedabad, Noida ITMS, Chandigarh ICCC, Ahmedabad Police, RTGC Andhra Pradesh, showroom and access floor visuals.
- Convert repeated project-photo slides into immersive galleries with animated crop/pan, not separate slides.
- Use architectural visualization for system explanations: exploded room layers, console modules, access-floor routing, sightline cones and operator workflow zones.
- Do not use generic office interiors as hero visuals for a control-room pitch. Tech Interiors, ceiling/cladding and fit-out visuals should appear only as supporting capabilities.

### Data and proof display

- Group facts should appear as a “scale foundation”: 1976, 1,000,000 sq. ft., 2,500+ people, 75+ countries served, 25 patents, 920 Cr. FY 2023-24.
- Workspace facts should appear as the specialist proof layer: 2006, 170,000 sq. ft. dedicated factory, 500+ customers, 30+ engineered products, 209 Cr. FY 2023-24, 10+ international certifications with certification names pending confirmation.
- Project values should be presented only in Presenter Mode or protected proof view unless explicitly approved for public use.

## Interaction principles

### Product exploration

- The main product interaction should be a control-room system explorer.
- Users can move between layers: Room, Console, Operator Ergonomics, Video Wall, Access Floor, Lighting/HVAC, Materials/Cladding.
- Hotspots should reveal short technical explanations, not marketing paragraphs.
- Console modules should be inspectable: monitor mounts, cable access, operator desk, lighting accents, CPU/power/data zones and seating/posture relationship.
- The access-floor interaction should reveal cable voids and serviceability, connecting page 26 and 27 content to the control-room system story.

### Project exploration

- Project references should be mapped by sector and geography.
- Featured projects should have cinematic entries: WDFCC Ahmedabad, Noida ITMS, Chandigarh ICCC, Ahmedabad Police, RTGC Andhra Pradesh, ISRO/DRDO, Dangote Refinery.
- Each project view should show only confirmed information: project name, location, sector, images, and value if approved.
- If outcome, timeline, operator count or scope is unavailable, the interface should avoid placeholders and instead use “Project details available on request” or omit the field.

### Trust exploration

- Customer logos should be grouped by sector, not shown as a wall first.
- Example groupings: Rail & Metro, Government & Defense, Oil & Gas, Utilities, Airports, Industry & Technology.
- A full logo wall can exist as an appendix/proof drawer.
- Tie-ups should open as partner capability cards: Schneider Electric, Honeywell, L&T and Thales, with exact claim language flagged for confirmation before publishing.

### Presenter controls

- Presenter controls should be discreet: previous, next, chapter map, proof drawer, narration toggle, fullscreen, mode switch.
- Keyboard and remote support: next, previous, open chapter map, close overlay, play/pause auto-play.
- A presenter should be able to jump from any chapter to “Projects,” “Product System,” “Company Proof” or “Contact.”

## Voiceover strategy

### Voice and tone

- Voice should be calm, confident, technical and premium.
- Avoid sales hype. The voice should sound like an expert guide walking a buyer through a mission-critical environment.
- Use short lines with pauses that match visual transitions.
- Narration should reinforce what is visible, not read screen text.

### Narration by mode

- Presenter Mode: narration off by default; optional chapter intro voice clips can be triggered manually.
- Self-Guided Mode: narration optional with visible mute/unmute and captions.
- Auto-Play Mode: narration on by default with timed subtitles/captions.

### Voiceover content approach

- Opening narration should frame control rooms as spaces where visibility, coordination and response matter.
- Company narration should mention only confirmed facts from the PDF.
- Product narration should explain the room as an integrated system: console, sightline, access floor, lighting, HVAC and workflow.
- Project narration should be factual: “WDFCC Ahmedabad,” “Noida ITMS,” “Chandigarh ICCC Smart City,” etc., with no invented outcomes.
- Awards and tie-ups narration should use cautious wording until claims are confirmed.

### Captions

- All narration must have captions.
- Captions should be large enough for showroom displays and should not cover product hotspots or project imagery.
- Auto-play captions should be concise and timed to visual beats.

## Animation strategy

### Motion character

- Motion should feel engineered: smooth, precise, weighted and intentional.
- Use slow camera moves, depth transitions, system reveals, line scans, lighting fades and layer expansions.
- Avoid bouncy UI, generic parallax, excessive particle effects and decorative animations.

### Cinematic transitions

- Chapter transitions should behave like moving through zones of a control room: exterior/factory, briefing wall, operator floor, system cutaway, project map, proof wall, showroom.
- Use 3D/2.5D camera moves where possible: dolly into console, orbit around workstation, elevate above room plan, descend through access floor.
- Data should animate as instrumentation: numbers count once, charts draw cleanly, map pins appear by region/sector.

### Functional animation

- Animation must teach the buyer something: sightline cones, cable flow, airflow/HVAC zones, access-floor service paths, task-analysis zones.
- Product explosions should be slow enough for a presenter to explain.
- In Auto-Play Mode, animations should complete without requiring interaction.

### Reduced motion

- Provide a reduced-motion mode that replaces camera moves with fades, stills and simple reveal states.
- Reduced-motion mode must still preserve chapter order, narration and all content.

## Navigation model

### Core structure

The experience should use a chapter rail, not a website navbar.

- The chapter rail can collapse into small illuminated markers.
- Current chapter, elapsed progress and mode should always be visible but understated.
- A central “Control Map” overlay lets users jump to any chapter, sector, project or product layer.
- Proof drawers provide additional details without breaking the main story.

### Customer-controlled navigation

- Users can move linearly, jump by chapter, explore a project map, or inspect product layers.
- No interaction should require precision hover only; touchscreen behavior must be first-class.
- Every overlay must have a visible close/back control and support Escape/back gesture.

### Mode switching

- Mode switcher should be available from the control panel: Presenter, Self-Guided, Auto-Play.
- Switching modes should keep the current chapter when possible.
- Auto-Play should show a countdown/progress indicator and a clear pause control.

## Recommended chapter sequence

1. **Mission Critical by Design**  
   Opens with a cinematic control-room environment and frames the importance of operator visibility, response and coordination.

2. **Pyrotech Workspace**  
   Introduces Pyrotech Workspace Solutions as the specialist control-room and workspace arm, backed by Pyrotech Group scale.

3. **Scale You Can Trust**  
   Presents confirmed group and workspace metrics: 1976, 1,000,000 sq. ft., 2,500+ people, 75+ countries, 25 patents, 2006, 170,000 sq. ft., 500+ customers, 209 Cr.

4. **The Room as a System**  
   Interactive control-room system model: consoles, video wall, operator ergonomics, lighting/HVAC, access floor, cable routing and interior envelope.

5. **Design-Build Method**  
   Shows task analysis, ergonomic study, space engineering, sightlines, lux levels and HVAC calculations as a guided process. Technical standard names are confirmation required.

6. **400+ Delivered Solutions**  
   Turns the existing “400+ Design to Built Solutions Delivered” claim into a premium proof moment, including design exclusivity and proposal-to-delivery fidelity.

7. **Flagship Control Rooms**  
   Immersive project gallery: WDFCC Ahmedabad, Noida ITMS, Chandigarh ICCC Smart City, Ahmedabad Police, RTGC Andhra Pradesh.

8. **Critical Sector Proof**  
   Sector navigation for Rail & Metro, Smart Cities, Government/Defense, Oil & Gas, Utilities, Airports/ATC, Industry/Technology.

9. **Global Delivery Footprint**  
   Interactive world map using the PDF’s “more than 200 customers from 25 different countries” design-build claim. Clarify relationship to 500+ customers and 75+ countries served.

10. **Awards, Partners, Approvals**  
   DRDO, ISRO and Thales recognition; Schneider Electric, Honeywell, L&T and Thales tie-up cards. Partner claim approvals are confirmation required.

11. **Showroom Experience**  
   Invites the buyer into the real showroom/demo environment, with optional 360-style exploration if assets are available.

12. **Start Your Control Room**  
   CTA: request a control-room audit, schedule a showroom visit, discuss a sector-specific reference or contact Pyrotech Workspace.

## Desktop behavior

- Desktop layout should support salesperson-led conversation on laptop or meeting-room display.
- Mouse, keyboard and remote-clicker controls must work cleanly.
- Desktop can show richer overlays: presenter notes, proof drawer, chapter map and project details.
- Avoid scroll-heavy pages. Each chapter should behave like a scene with internal interactions, not a long webpage.
- For sales calls, include a low-bandwidth mode that preloads chapter stills and skips heavy 3D if needed.

## Touchscreen behavior

- Touch targets must be large and spaced for showroom use.
- All product hotspots should open on tap, not hover.
- Drag gestures should rotate/explore product models or architectural scenes.
- Pinch-to-zoom should work on project images, maps and system diagrams.
- Idle timeout should return from deep detail overlays to the current chapter, then eventually to ambient auto-play.
- A visitor should be able to explore without reading instructions. Interaction affordances must be visible through familiar controls, motion cues and highlighted hotspots.

## Large-display behavior

- Large-display mode should be legible from across a showroom or boardroom.
- Text must be minimal and oversized; narration and captions carry the detailed story.
- UI controls should be hidden or minimized during Auto-Play Mode, then appear when someone interacts.
- On ultra-wide displays, the experience should preserve cinematic framing rather than stretching content awkwardly.
- Presenter Mode on a large display should support a secondary private controller view if developed later, but the primary display must still work standalone.

## Accessibility requirements

- Full keyboard navigation for chapters, overlays, hotspots and mode controls.
- Captions for every voiceover segment.
- Transcript available for the full Auto-Play narration.
- Reduced-motion mode for users sensitive to cinematic camera movement.
- Sufficient contrast for dark control-room UI and amber/yellow accent elements.
- Do not rely on color alone to identify sectors, active states or warnings.
- Touch targets should be at least 44px equivalent, larger for showroom displays.
- All meaningful images need alt text or equivalent labels, especially project images and product-layer diagrams.
- Auto-play must be pausable.
- Audio should never start unexpectedly in Self-Guided or Presenter Mode.

## Performance requirements

- The experience must feel immediate in a showroom. First meaningful visual should appear within 2 seconds on the target hardware after assets are cached.
- Heavy scenes should preload the next chapter while the current chapter plays.
- Use progressive asset loading: hero still first, then video/3D/interactive layers.
- Keep animations at a stable 60fps on the target showroom machine; provide fallback stills for lower-end devices.
- Optimize project images into responsive formats; do not ship the raw PDF slide images as the final presentation assets.
- 3D models, if used, should be compressed and tested on touchscreen hardware.
- Narration audio should be chapter-based, not one monolithic file, so Presenter and Self-Guided modes can jump instantly.
- The full Auto-Play loop should run for hours without memory growth, audio drift or interaction lag.

## Offline presentation requirements

- The experience must run fully offline for showroom, client visits and exhibitions.
- All images, audio, fonts, 3D assets, captions and project data must be bundled locally.
- No internet dependency for customer logos, maps, analytics, fonts, video playback or narration.
- Provide a local launch method suitable for sales teams: desktop shortcut, local server script or packaged app.
- Auto-Play Mode should recover after refresh, display sleep or accidental reload.
- Include a configuration file for mode defaults: start mode, narration on/off, idle timeout, protected proof visibility and chapter loop duration.
- Include an asset manifest so sales teams can verify all required assets are present before travelling.
- If any content is approval-sensitive, support an offline “public-safe” profile that hides project values, full logo wall and partner claims until enabled.

## Content governance requirements

- Do not invent company facts beyond the PDF analysis.
- Facts requiring confirmation must remain marked internally until verified: certification names, partner claim language, ISRO/Thales award details, RTGC naming, access-floor technical specs, service/warranty details and public-use permissions.
- Customer logos should be stored as approved assets, not scraped from the web at runtime.
- Each project should have a small structured record: name, sector, location, approved images, approved value, approved description, public/private visibility.
- The experience should support future updates without redesigning the whole system: add a project, update a metric, hide a logo, swap narration, change the chapter order.

## What this experience should not do

- It should not recreate the PDF as horizontal slides.
- It should not become a corporate website with hero, cards, sections and generic service copy.
- It should not lead with a dense logo wall.
- It should not make unrelated interiors feel equal to control-room solutions.
- It should not publish project values, partner claims or customer logos without approval.
- It should not use generic futuristic visuals that do not explain Pyrotech’s actual control-room offering.
